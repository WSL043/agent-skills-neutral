# Formulate Scientific Hypotheses

## Goal

Turn an observation into a bounded research question and a set of explanations that can be distinguished by evidence.

## Workflow

1. Freeze the observation before interpreting it. Record the evidence boundary, dates, population or system scope, measurement context, and what is directly observed versus inferred.
2. State one research question and identify the claim type: descriptive, associational, predictive, causal, or mechanistic. For causal claims, define the estimand, assumptions, intervention or exposure, and outcome.
3. Generate rival explanatory classes, including a mechanism, measurement artifact, confounding, selection or collider bias, reverse causation, stochastic variation, and context dependence when they are plausible. Do not collapse alternatives into a single favored story.
4. For each serious hypothesis, derive predictions that differ from its rivals, results that would count against it, and outcomes that would remain indeterminate. Make the relevant variables and observations operational.
5. Match the design and analysis to the claim. Check measurement validity, bias, missingness, dependence, alternative explanations, ethics, safety, authorization, and human accountability before collecting or acting on data.
6. Record the planned analysis, deviations, replication or reproducibility path, and evidence needed to update the conclusion. Keep exploratory findings separate from confirmatory claims.

## Decision rules

- A hypothesis is useful only when its mechanism and predictions could be wrong in an observable way.
- Use primary evidence and current scope boundaries; a plausible narrative is not a causal result.
- Prefer discriminating observations that separate rivals over additional detail about one preferred explanation.
- State whether a result supports, qualifies, contradicts, or leaves a hypothesis unresolved.

## Guardrails

- Do not turn post-hoc patterns into pre-registered claims or hide analysis changes.
- Do not invent universal sample, significance, or confidence thresholds; justify design choices for the claim and context.
- Do not provide unsafe clinical, biological, or dual-use operational instructions; escalate decisions requiring qualified human oversight.

## Completion evidence

- Observation, question, claim type, hypotheses, mechanisms, predictions, measurements, assumptions, and alternatives are explicit.
- The plan identifies results that would distinguish or fail to distinguish the alternatives.
- Evidence limits, ethical boundaries, reproducibility steps, and unresolved uncertainty are visible.
