# Dated generator fingerprints

This file tracks recurring visual shortcuts observed across unrelated generated designs. It is intentionally separate from durable design judgment because model behavior, popular templates, and visual trends change.

**Observation date:** 2026-08-07

## How to use this file

A fingerprint is evidence of generic generation only when it appears without a brief-specific reason. None of the examples below is banned. A cream editorial page, a bento grid, glass, a purple gradient, or a centered hero can all be correct when the subject and composition actually call for them.

Do not write "this looks AI-generated" as a finding. Name the mechanism:

- the same motif appears across unrelated briefs;
- a visual device has no content, interaction, material, or narrative role;
- several fashionable devices are correlated in the same familiar bundle;
- surface novelty masks an unchanged template skeleton;
- copy, labels, metrics, or diagrams imitate specificity without carrying real information.

## Currently recurring clusters

These are examples for calibration, not defaults or permanent anti-patterns.

- centered hero copy over a dark mesh, glow, aurora, or radial-gradient field;
- warm cream background + high-contrast display serif + muted terracotta or earthy accent, regardless of subject;
- near-black background + one acid green, vermilion, or electric accent used as an automatic "edgy" identity;
- broadsheet/editorial styling with hairline rules, dense columns, numbering, or newspaper furniture that does not encode real editorial structure;
- three equal feature cards, repeated bento tiles, or a page made almost entirely from same-weight rounded containers;
- excessive pills, tiny eyebrow labels, rounded badges, browser chrome, or pseudo-system tags used to manufacture hierarchy;
- glassmorphism, blur, glow, and gradient borders applied to every surface instead of describing a coherent material model;
- a random italic or serif word inserted into an otherwise unrelated display face solely to signal "premium" or "creative";
- fake product metrics, meaningless charts, invented technical labels, sequence numbers, coordinate marks, or microcopy that exists only to make a composition look designed;
- identical fade/slide/float reveals on every section, springiness on every interaction, or ambient motion that continues after it stops communicating state;
- oversized type plus enormous whitespace used as a substitute for hierarchy rather than as a deliberate scale relationship;
- identical card radius, shadow, border, and spacing grammar applied to content with very different semantic roles.

## Model- and era-aware review

When the generating model or tool is known, compare the work with other recent outputs from that model before attributing a motif to it. A model-specific fingerprint should be based on recurrence across unrelated prompts, not a single memorable example.

Record new observations in plain visual terms. If useful, note the model family or generation period, but do not guess them from appearance alone. When the model is unknown, say unknown.

A fingerprint should be revised or removed when:

- it no longer recurs across current generated work;
- it has become a normal, intentional convention and no longer signals template substitution by itself;
- a newer model exhibits a different correlated default;
- evidence shows the pattern was actually domain-driven rather than generator-driven.

## Evolution test

Periodically compare recent independent design skills and generated examples against this file. Promote only the underlying durable lesson into `aesthetic-judgment.md`; keep transient examples here.

The goal is not to make AI avoid whatever AI did last year. The goal is to make the agent notice when it is choosing from habit instead of from the brief.
